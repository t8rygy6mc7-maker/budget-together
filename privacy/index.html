Privacy Policy for Even Keel

Effective date: August 3, 2026
Last updated: November 11, 2026

The short version

Even Keel has no servers. There is no account to create, nothing to sign up for, and no way for us to see what you spend. Everything you type stays on your iPhone and, if you have iCloud switched on, in your own iCloud account.

We do not collect, receive, store, sell, or share your personal information. We have no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no third-party code of any kind in the app.

Who this covers

This policy covers the Even Keel iPhone app published by Ren ("we", "us"). If you have a question about it, write to budgetog@icloud.com.

What the app stores

Even Keel keeps whatever you choose to put into it:

  • Transactions — amount, date, place or description, category, and whether it is a spend or income
  • Optional notes and a mood tag on a transaction
  • The names and colours you give the people in your budget
  • Spending limits, recurring items, loans, savings goals, and challenges you set up
  • Your app settings, such as appearance and whether the simplified view is on

You never have to give us a name, an email address, a phone number, or a date of birth, because there is nowhere to send them.

Where it is stored

On your device. The app's records live in a database inside the app's own storage on your iPhone. That file is protected by iOS file encryption and cannot be read until the phone has been unlocked at least once since it was switched on.

Two limits on that worth knowing, because they are the difference between what file encryption does and what people often assume it does. It protects the file against someone who has your powered-off phone; it does not protect it against someone using your phone while it is unlocked — for that, turn on the app lock described under Your controls. And if you back your iPhone up to a computer without ticking "Encrypt local backup", the backup copy is not encrypted, so the safest thing is to leave that option on. Backups to iCloud are encrypted.

In your iCloud. If you are signed into iCloud and have iCloud Drive turned on, the app mirrors your budget into your own private iCloud database using Apple's CloudKit. This is what keeps your budget in step across your own devices and lets you share a budget with someone else.

That iCloud data belongs to your Apple Account. It sits in Apple's infrastructure under Apple's terms and privacy policy, not ours. We have no access to it. We cannot read it, export it, or recover it for you. Notes and mood tags are additionally stored in CloudKit's encrypted fields.

If you are not signed into iCloud, or you turn iCloud off for Even Keel in iOS Settings, the app keeps working and your budget simply stays on that one device.

Sharing a budget with other people

When you invite someone to a budget, the app creates a private iCloud share and gives you a link to send them however you like. We never see the link and are not involved in sending it.

Anyone who accepts the invitation can see the budget's transactions, the people in it, the limits, and any notes or reactions on shared entries — the same things you can see. They can add and edit entries too. Only invite people you would be comfortable showing your spending to.

You can stop sharing at any time from the People screen. Removing someone revokes their access; if you leave a budget someone else owns, your copy is removed from your device and your iCloud.

What "private" means

Marking an entry private hides it from the other people in your budget. They will not see the amount, the place, the note, or that it exists.

It does not mean the entry stays only on this device. A private entry is still stored in your own private iCloud database along with the rest of your budget, so it will appear on your own other devices. It is private from other people, not from your Apple Account.

Private entries are also included in any file you export — see below.

Microphone and speech recognition

If you use the voice option to enter an amount, the app asks for permission to use the microphone and to use speech recognition.

The app forces on-device recognition. Your speech is turned into text by your iPhone itself and is not sent to Apple's servers or anywhere else for processing. Audio is not recorded, saved, or kept — only the amount it recognised is used, and only to fill in the field you were typing into. You can withdraw either permission at any time in iOS Settings; the rest of the app carries on working.

Notifications

The app can send you local reminders about your budget. These are scheduled and delivered by your own iPhone. No content is sent through any server, and we are not told whether you read them.

Separately, the app registers for silent background notifications from iCloud. These carry no content and are never shown to you. Their only job is to let your phone know that one of your other devices — or someone you share a budget with — has changed something, so the budget updates promptly instead of waiting for the next time you open the app.

Exporting your data

You can export your budget to a file at any time. The file is written to temporary storage on your device and handed to the iOS share sheet, so it goes wherever you send it. Once you send it somewhere, it is governed by whatever you sent it to, not by this policy. Copies left in temporary storage are cleaned up automatically.

Exports include private entries, because a backup that quietly dropped rows would not be a backup. The export screen tells you when private entries are in the file.

What we do not do

To be specific about it, Even Keel does not:

  • Connect to your bank or any financial institution
  • Ask for or handle card numbers, account numbers, or credentials
  • Contain any analytics, crash reporting, advertising, or attribution code
  • Contain any third-party SDK or library
  • Make any network request to any server we operate — we operate none
  • Track you across apps or websites, or share anything for tracking purposes
  • Sell or disclose personal information to anybody
  • Show ads or contain in-app purchases

Because we receive nothing, there is nothing for us to profile you with, and there is no scenario in which we hand your data to anyone.

Children

Even Keel does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, children included, because it does not collect personal information at all. It is not directed at children, and it has no chat, no social features, and no external links to unmoderated content.

Your controls

  • Lock the app — the Data screen can require Face ID, Touch ID, or your passcode before the app will open, at launch and again whenever it has been closed for more than a minute. It is off unless you turn it on. This is a door on the app rather than a second lock on the data: it stops someone who picks up your unlocked phone, and the file underneath is encrypted with your device passcode either way
  • See everything the app holds — export it from the Data screen
  • Delete one entry — swipe it away in the Log
  • Delete everything — the Data screen has an erase option that destroys the budget on that device and in your iCloud, guarded by typing a confirmation. It cannot be undone
  • Stop syncing — turn Even Keel off under iCloud in iOS Settings
  • Withdraw microphone or speech permission — iOS Settings, at any time
  • Remove the app — deleting it removes its local storage. Data already in your iCloud is managed through your Apple Account settings

Because we hold no data about you, requests to access, correct, port, or delete "your data held by us" have nothing to act on — everything is already in your hands. Residents of regions with data protection laws such as the GDPR or the CCPA retain their rights; they simply resolve to the controls above. We do not sell or share personal information, so there is no opt-out to offer.

Data breaches

We hold no user data, so there is no store of it for us to lose. Any incident involving iCloud itself would be Apple's to handle under Apple's own policies.

Changes to this policy

If the app ever changes in a way that affects this policy, we will update it here and change the date at the top. If a change is significant, we will say so in the app's release notes rather than quietly editing the page.
